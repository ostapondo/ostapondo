Cube and colour · the product

The app,
same as the site.

One idea carries all of it: colour is the zone number. Zone 1 is always rose, 2 plum, 3 blue, 4 mint. So the menu bar grid, the drag overlay, the editor, the desks and ⌃⌥1–9 all say the same thing twice.

That turns the site's decoration into the app's information — which is the only honest reason to put gradients in a utility.

Two things make this README carry the brand where GitHub gives you no stylesheet: the banner is an animated GIF built from the site's own CSS, so it can never drift out of sync with the design, and the four coloured cards are one SVG that reads the same in GitHub's light theme and its dark one. The badge colours are the zone colours, in order.

What I would check before building any of this. Translucency in the app is free on macOS, but a gradient behind every zone is drawn on every drag frame — worth measuring on an Intel Mac before committing, and easy to drop to flat fills that keep the same hues. And the colour-as-number rule only holds up to about six zones; past that the hues stop being distinguishable and the number has to carry it alone.
